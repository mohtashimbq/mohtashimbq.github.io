How I approach research

Method before spectacle.

Useful research needs more than a strong model.

I tend to work from the measurement problem outward: what is being represented, what assumptions are hidden in the data, how the model is evaluated, and whether the chosen metric reflects the real objective.

This is particularly important in image quality assessment, where numerical agreement and perceptual agreement are not always the same thing. It also carries into applied machine learning and connected systems, where deployment constraints can make a technically elegant method impractical.

Current collaborations are broadening that perspective into edge intelligence, digital twins and Internet of Medical Things research.

Current directions

Questions I am actively exploring

These are active directions rather than claims of completed outcomes.

Cross-content image quality assessment

How can a quality metric remain useful across natural-scene and screen-content imagery without becoming overly specialised to one content type?

Perceptually guided reconstruction

How can reconstruction and enhancement objectives better align with perceptual image-quality behaviour?

Intelligence at the network edge

How can learning systems operate under latency, resource and reliability constraints in connected environments?

AI for connected health

How can digital twins, IoMT and intelligent resource allocation support more responsive connected-health systems?
